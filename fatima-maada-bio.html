The First Lady

A life that became a public mission

Her Excellency Dr. Fatima Maada Bio's advocacy is deeply connected to her own story: a girl who narrowly escaped child marriage, began philanthropic work while still young, built a professional life in London and the African film industry, and later turned the platform of First Lady into a sustained campaign for women and girls.

Her formal education includes studies in Performing Arts at Roehampton and Journalism at the London College of Communication. Before public life, she worked as an actress, scriptwriter and executive producer, with prominent screen work including The Mirror Boy. That combination of personal experience, communications training, creative work and philanthropy has shaped a public style built around visibility, direct engagement and advocacy.

Continental Leadership

President of Africa's First Ladies development platform

In February 2025, Dr. Fatima Maada Bio was elected President of the Organisation of African First Ladies for Development at its General Assembly during the African Union Summit in Addis Ababa. Her two-year mandate places her at the head of a continental platform focused on women, children, health and development — expanding the scale of her advocacy from a national campaign in Sierra Leone to agenda-setting and partnership-building among First Ladies across Africa.

Signature Public Legacy

Advocacy translated into national action

Six areas that define the public record behind her keynote appearance.

Protection of Girls

Hands Off Our Girls

Launched in 2018 as a high-visibility campaign against rape, child marriage and violence against women and girls, later carried to international platforms including the United Nations and Women Deliver.

Menstrual Dignity

Free sanitary pads for schoolgirls

A nationwide initiative designed to reduce menstruation-related absenteeism, support school retention and normalize public discussion of menstrual health and hygiene.

Water, Sanitation & Hygiene

AU sanitation and hygiene champion

Her advocacy around sanitation, menstrual hygiene and access to water was formally recognized in 2021 when she was named a champion for sanitation and hygiene.

Education

UN Education Plus champion

In 2021 she was recognized as a champion of the United Nations Education Plus initiative, reinforcing the link between girls' education, protection, health and opportunity.

Women, Peace & Security

Women in peacekeeping

In 2025 she launched a Sierra Leone initiative with the armed forces and UN Women aimed at strengthening women's participation and leadership in peacekeeping operations.

Social Protection

National food-basket response

During the COVID-19 period, her office organized food-basket support reaching districts across Sierra Leone, extending her advocacy into emergency social relief.

A Public Journey

Milestones in advocacy and leadership

A selective timeline of the moments that transformed a personal cause into national and international leadership.

2018

Hands Off Our Girls takes shape

A Freetown march against gender-based violence involving First Ladies from other African countries helped launch the campaign as a signature initiative of the Office of the First Lady.

2019

National and global attention intensifies

The campaign became part of a broader national mobilization around sexual violence as Sierra Leone declared a national emergency and strengthened its sexual-offences framework. The campaign was also presented at a high-level event during the UN General Assembly in New York.

2019

Free sanitary pads initiative launched

The schoolgirl sanitary-pad programme linked menstrual dignity to education, attendance and the broader Free Quality Education agenda.

2021

International champion roles

She was recognized as a sanitation and hygiene champion and as a UN Education Plus champion, expanding the international profile of her work on girls' welfare and education.

2024

Honorary Doctor of Letters

Regional Maritime University in Accra awarded her an honorary Doctor of Letters for contributions including gender advocacy, democracy, social justice and equal opportunity.

2025

Elected President of OAFLAD

African First Ladies elected her to a two-year term leading the Organisation of African First Ladies for Development.

2025

Women repositioned for peacekeeping

She launched a partnership supporting greater participation and leadership of Sierra Leonean women in the armed forces and UN peace operations.

Beyond Public Office

Personal story, professional formation and recognition

The breadth of her profile reaches beyond the formal role of First Lady.

Personal & Professional

Communicator, creative professional, philanthropist

Her official biography describes early philanthropic work, later study and charity work in London, and a career in entertainment as an actress, scriptwriter and executive producer. Her screen career included The Mirror Boy, while her journalism and performing-arts training helped develop the public-facing communications style that now defines much of her advocacy.

Her own account of narrowly escaping child marriage gives the protection of girls a personal dimension that predates her years in public office.

Three questions her record brings to New York

How does advocacy become durable change?

Public visibility matters most when it creates institutions, laws, services, partnerships and cultural change that outlast a campaign moment.

For the incoming executive

The same question applies to chapter leadership: how will a new mandate become measurable service?

How do leaders protect those with the least power?

Her signature work focuses attention on girls, survivors of violence and communities facing barriers to dignity, safety and education.

For the incoming executive

Leadership is tested by inclusion, protection and whether the organization creates space for members who are easiest to overlook.

How does diaspora leadership connect to Africa?

Her continental role underscores the importance of networks that cross borders while remaining grounded in communities and national priorities.
